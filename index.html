Hello Web Visitors :]

My website is a shifting house next to a river of knowledge. What could yours be?

The article had me thinking more about websites on a personal level rather than an academic or surface level definition of what they are. When learning about creating websites, we often only think of the technical details of what makes a website rather than what a website is to us. A website can mean many things, it is seen a lot of the time as a space one uses to share about themselves, like with our passion projects, or it can be a space that tells a story almost like in this case, a house. Just like we decorate a house with furniture, we decorate a website with font colors, sizes, background colors, images and much more. Also wanting to kind of point out something outside of the story I thought could work, but along with a river, websites can also be boats, sharing knowledge and insp[iration to other web designers.

A Rant About "Technology"

“Its technology is how a society copes with physical reality: how people get and keep and cook food, how they clothe themselves, what their power sources are (animal? human? water? wind? electricity? other?) what they build with and what they build, their medicine - and so on and on.” A quote that resonated with me from this short web essay. I feel like as of now and as we’ve progressed and advanced technologically that we have become completely reliant on it. It’s how we even do 90% of tasks nowadays, because everything has become easier if you do it with the internet or on the web. Hell, even the way we use our time on the internet has become a way to cope with how shitty the world has gotten. Think about doom scrolling or listening to music so loud you can’t hear the world or even hear yourself think.

"A handmade Web by J.R Carpenter"

I like the drawback to the overall history of the handmade web and handmade webpages. I feel like we draw so much from future engagement and what it means to design a webpage or design for the internet today, that we fail to look back on the internet before to see where we are and where we can improve. I’m not saying we need to go back to the way the internet was, but it is interesting to see the differences from the past to now and what we can see or make for the future. Also want to point out this quote. “Ironically, handheld devices have distanced us from the handmade web. In Reading Writing Interfaces: From the Digital to the Bookbound (2014), Lori Emerson argues: "The iPad works because users can’t know how it works" (15). Reading the web on an iPhone, iPad, or similar device, readers do not have the option of viewing the page source.” Thought it was interesting.
